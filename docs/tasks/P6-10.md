## Goal

Prove the phase: run the cross-check over every corpus formula and report
the agreement rate with cached values (ADR 0059), and pin the exit
criterion in a test — resizing a master-based corpus shape recomputes its
geometry correctly for supported functions.

## Interfaces

No new public interfaces: both files are integration tests over the
committed API. The cross-check prints the ADR 0059 block on stdout
(`-- --nocapture` shows it) and asserts every counted cell agrees within
tolerance; any accepted divergence goes into ADR 0059's exclusion list
(you may append to that ADR's "Documented exclusions" section — one line
each, with the reason).

## Master notes

- P6-02 merged. 976 of the 994 unique corpus formulas parse; the 18
  that do not are `FORMATEX` calls with omitted arguments
  (`FORMATEX(a,b,,,c)`), which the parser rejects by decision. Count
  them under their own skip reason ("does not parse") in the cross-check;
  they keep their cached values.

- P6-07 merged. Resize goes through the registry: `shape.resize` (or a
  `CellEdit` on `Width`/`Height`) then `push_edit` recalculates the
  dirty cells with `materialise_inherited`. Corpus instances carry
  their own `Inh` rows, so the exit test is not affected by P6-07 open
  issue 2 (an instance with no own geometry rows cannot be
  materialised). Ferrogram's own catalog masters have constant geometry
  and still scale literally; do not use them for the exit test.
- The fill-in entry point is `commands::recalc::fill_missing`.
